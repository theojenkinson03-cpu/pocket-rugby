        How to play

        The aim

        Two ways to play. On the clock: a set number of dice rolls (60, 100 or 160), half each way, and the higher score wins. When a half's rolls run out it's the last play: play goes on until the ball is dead (a score, an error, a turnover or a kick at goal). A draw can happen; in a knockout match it goes to sudden death, where the next score of any kind wins.

        Race: first to 30 points (20 or 50 in shorter or longer matches), with half time when either side reaches half that. The match ends the moment a side reaches the full-time score, even before a conversion.

        Attacking

        Your team is five dice. The Scrum Half always rolls first, after the kick-off, after every tackle and whenever you win the ball. Most of its faces pass to a named player, who rolls their own die next.

        A ‹Pass› face lets you choose the Forwards, Centres, Outside Backs or Fly Half. A run shows an intensity number: the higher it is, the harder it is to stop. Each has one big move: Forwards Line Break 6, Centres Thru the Gap 7, Outside Backs Into the Corner 8, Fly Half High Ball 6. (The printed game calls them the Forward, Centre, Wing and Back.)

        The dice guide: before every roll, the row under the dice shows what each face would do, with injuries, cards and the weather counted. Green is good for you, red is bad, grey is neither; tap a face for more. A badge in a die's corner shows anything changing its numbers: an injury (−1), the sin bin (−1), a red card (−2), advantage (+1), the wind or a team card.

        Auto-roll rolls your dice for you and stops at every decision. Turn it on and off with the Auto button above the Roll button.

        Tackling

        The defence rolls the brown die twice and adds the numbers. Reach the intensity and the tackle is made; play goes back to the Scrum Half. Fall short and it's a line break: the attacker rolls the black die. TRY scores 5, NO TRY is a changeover (so is RUCK WIN: held up over the line), VIDEO REF means roll again.

        Big Hit on the first brown die against a plain Hit Up of 3 or 4 (not a big move) sets up a ruck contest: the defence rolls the black die and steals the ball on RUCK WIN. Big Hit on the second die always completes the tackle. Two Big Hits injure the ball carrier: that position runs at −1 for the rest of the match.

        Kicking

        Roll the brown die for the range, then the black die's number for the kick. The kick must be at least the range. Each number is 10 metres on the pitch.

        The Fly Half kicks every goal. Every try is followed by a conversion. The Fly Half's die and the Centres' die have a Drop Goal face: on the Centres' die the Centre gives it back to the Fly Half, who has the chance. Check the range first, then decide whether to kick or pass. A missed drop goal or penalty kick hands the ball over.

        Errors and set pieces

        A Knock On gives the other side a scrum; Into Touch gives them a lineout. They roll the white die: a green light wins the ball, red loses it. A collapsed scrum is reset and rolled again. A lineout that's not straight passes the throw to the other side. Intercepted turns the ball over straight away.

        Penalties and cards

        The white die can award a penalty either way. The black die then checks for a card: the yellow dot sends a player to the sin bin and the offenders take −1 off each brown die they roll in a tackle for the rest of the half. A second yellow makes it −2 a die for the rest of the match.

        The range for the kick is rolled at the same time, so you know the distance before you choose: kick for goal (3 points), or quick tap for +1 intensity on your next 3 runs. The advantage ends after 3 tackles or an error.

        Restarts

        After any score the side that conceded gets the ball back from the restart, so a lead is never safe. (The printed game gives it to the scorers; over 2,000 simulated matches that let one side run away with too many games.) The coin toss decides who has the ball first; the other side starts the second half with it.

        Team cards

        With team cards on, five of the twelve cards are dealt after the coin toss and each side picks two. The side that lost the toss picks 1st and 4th; the side that won it (and has the first ball) picks 2nd and 3rd. The fifth card is discarded. Both cards last the whole match and work together.

        Every card is dealt at a rarity: Common (55% of cards), Rare (28%), Epic (13%) or Mythic (4%, so about one deal in five has a Mythic, and it can be the first card you pick). The rarer the card, the stronger it is: each rarity keeps everything below it and adds more. Within a rarity the cards were balanced over thousands of simulated matches, so pick the one that suits your style, or the rarest one on the table.

        Weather and the bench

        Two optional rules, switched on or off like the team cards.

        Weather: every match gets its own conditions. In the rain the ball is slippery: the Forwards' and Centres' free ‹Pass› face is a knock-on. Their runs, and the Scrum Half's passes, are as normal. Wind is worth 10 m on every kick: further with it at your back, shorter into it. The ends change at half time, so the wind swaps sides.

        The bench: once a half, each side can bring on a replacement for an injured player, which clears the injury. You choose when someone is hurt (any injured player can come off then) and at half time, when each side gets a fresh replacement. Use it now, or keep it in case someone more important gets hurt; an unused one is gone at half time.

        The CPU

        Rookie plays on instinct. Pro plays the odds. Expert plays out hundreds of continuations before every decision and picks the one that wins most often. In a dice game the dice decide most matches, so Expert is only a little stronger than Pro, but it rarely makes a poor call. The CPU's style flavours its choices: Boot it kicks at every chance, Run it taps penalties and spreads the ball wide, Forwards keeps it tight.

        Tournaments

        The World Cup is the real 2027 tournament in Australia: all 24 nations in the six pools of the draw. The top two in each pool and the four best third-placed sides go into the round of 16, then the real bracket to the final at Stadium Australia, Sydney. Or take a club through a real league, each with its real play-offs:

        • Super Rugby Pacific: 11 teams, one sitting out each round. The top six play qualifying finals; the three winners and the best-placed loser reach the semi-finals.
        • Prem Rugby: 10 clubs. The top four play semi-finals; the final is at Twickenham.
        • United Rugby Championship: 16 teams. The top eight play quarter-finals.
        • Top 14: 14 clubs. Third to sixth play barrages; the top two wait in the semi-finals, in Bordeaux, and the final is at the Stade de France.

        The Champions Cup has the 24 clubs from the 2026–27 draw in four pools of six. Each plays the four clubs in its pool from the other two leagues. The top three in each pool and the four best of the rest go into a round of 16, then quarter-finals, semi-finals and a final in Lyon.

        Everyone plays at their real home ground, except the neutral finals. When a league season ends you can carry on with your club into the next, squad and all, building a trophy cabinet and a records book; the top eight in each European league qualify for the next Champions Cup. The hub keeps the season's top try and points scorers and a Team of the Season.

        A short season is 6 rounds; a full season has everyone play everyone once. A win is worth 4 points and a draw 2, with a bonus point for winning by 10 or more or losing by 5 or fewer. Every side fields five of its real players. In matches you don't play, the stronger side has the edge.

        Online

        Play a friend on two phones. One of you hosts and shares a six-letter code; the other joins with it. Both of you need to open this same page signed in, with access to it. Each phone rolls its own dice and checks every move the other phone makes against the rules. Online, auto-roll starts on, and each decision has 10 seconds: run out of time and the other side gets a penalty (the toss and the card picks are made for you instead).

        In a friends league, each friend takes a club in the same league and the CPU plays the rest. Matches between friends are played online; a round moves on once every friend's match is played.

        The daily challenge is one situation a day, the same for everyone: your first attempt goes on the day's leaderboard. Both need the page on claude.ai, shared with your friends as Contributors.
