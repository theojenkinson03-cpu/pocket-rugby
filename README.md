# Pocket Rugby

A digital version of the Pocket Sports Rugby dice game, built for phones: quick to pick up, quick to play. Play the CPU, pass one phone between two players, or play a friend on two phones. Take a nation through the 2027 World Cup, a real club through a season of Super Rugby Pacific, Prem Rugby, the United Rugby Championship or the Top 14 (and on into the next, season after season), or a European club through the Champions Cup. Every side fields five of its real players.

Each team is five dice: the Scrum Half, Forwards, Centres, Outside Backs and Fly Half (the printed set calls the last four the Forward, Centre, Wing and Back). The defence, referee and scoring dice settle tackles, scrums, lineouts, penalties, tries and kicks. Play **on the clock** (a set number of rolls, the higher score wins) or a **race** (first to 30, half time at 15).

## Ways to play

- **1 player against the CPU.** Three levels: Rookie plays on instinct, Pro plays the odds, Expert plays out hundreds of continuations before each decision. Four styles: Balanced, Boot it (kicks at every chance), Run it (taps penalties and spreads it wide) and Forwards (keeps it tight).
- **2 players** on one phone, taking turns.
- **Online, on two phones.** One player hosts and gets a six-letter code; the other joins with it. See [Online play](#online-play) for what it needs.
- **World Cup 2027**: all 24 nations in the real pools, then a round of 16 and the real bracket to the final in Sydney (see [World Cup](#world-cup)).
- **Four real leagues**, each with its real teams and its own play-offs (see [Leagues](#leagues)). Play a short season of six rounds or a full one where everyone meets once.
- **Champions Cup**: the real 2026–27 draw, 24 clubs from the Prem, the URC and the Top 14 (see [Champions Cup](#champions-cup)).
- **Club career**: when a league season ends, carry on into the next with the same club and squad (see [Club career](#club-career)).
- **Daily challenge**: the same rigged situation for everyone each day, with a shared leaderboard and a score card to share (see [Daily challenge](#daily-challenge)).
- **Friends league**: several phones each take a club in the same league, and the CPU plays the rest (see [Friends league](#friends-league)).
- Tables use 4 points for a win and 2 for a draw, with a **bonus point for winning by 10 or more or losing by 5 or fewer** (not rugby's usual four-try bonus: the margin means the same in every format). Other fixtures are played out by the engine, CPU against CPU, with each side's own level and style, and the stronger side given the edge (see [Class](#class)).
- **Learn to play**: a guided first match against a Rookie, with a coach who explains each part of the game the first time it comes up. New players see it first on the title screen.

## Two ways to play a match

- **On the clock** (the default): a set number of dice rolls, half each way: **60** (Quick), **100** (Standard) or **160** (Marathon). The scoreboard counts the rolls left in the half. When they run out it's the **last play**: play goes on until the ball is dead (a score and its conversion, an error, a turnover, a kick at goal), then it's half time or full time, whatever the score. A draw can happen; in a knockout it goes to **sudden death**, the next score of any kind wins. A penalty on the last play can still be kicked or tapped.
- **Race**: first to 20, 30 or 50, with half time when a side reaches half that.

The roll counts come from simulation. Over 600 Pro-against-Pro matches at each length, races to 20, 30 and 50 took 133, 210 and 350 rolls on average, with a long tail (one in ten ran past 206, 314 and 483). On the clock the same lengths take 72, 112 and 172 rolls (last plays add about 11) and never much more: one in ten runs past 83, 125 and 185. Each side scores 9, 14 and 21 points on average, and 8%, 6% and 3% of matches are drawn.

## Class

Every side has a strength: the nations a rating from 1 (Chile, Hong Kong China, Zimbabwe) to 5 (New Zealand, South Africa, Ireland, France, England), the clubs one from their level. It only counts in the matches the CPU plays out, where the stronger side gets team cards for the gap (one Epic for a gap of one, up to two Mythics and two Epics for four): over 300 quick matches each, a gap of one won 61% to 68% of the time and a gap of four 85% to 95%. Your own matches are decided by the dice and your choices, whoever you play.

## Club career

When a league season ends, **Start the next season** carries on with the same club: every side keeps its squad (yours, edited or not, included), and the season name moves on (2026–27, 2027–28, ...; 2026, 2027, ... in Super Rugby). A club in the Champions Cup plays it after the league: the real draw in the first season, a fresh draw after that. The top eight in each European league's table qualify for the next season's Champions Cup.

The hub keeps the club's story:

- **Trophy cabinet**: league titles, Champions Cup wins, the Double, and finishing top of the table.
- **Club records**: biggest win, heaviest defeat, most points and tries in a match, the longest winning run, the best league season, and the most tries, points, metres and tackles by one player in a season.
- **All-time leaders**: tries, points and appearances for the club across every season.
- **Season by season**: table place, how far the club went in the play-offs and in Europe, the record and the top try scorer.

## Season stats

Every match in a competition counts, the ones the engine plays out as well as yours: every fixture keeps each player's numbers and the Player of the Match. The hub lists the top try scorers, top points scorers, most metres and most tackles, and a **Team of the Season**: the best average match rating at each position among the regulars (at least half as many matches as the most at that position), with a Player of the Season named when the competition ends.

## Daily challenge

One situation a day, the same for everyone (it changes at midnight UTC): two real clubs from one league, deep in the second half, with you behind and the other side close to the finish. "7 down, 5 to play" in a first-to-30 race means they need 5 more points to win and you trail by 7. The date also picks who has the ball (your ball, their ball, a penalty, a scrum or a lineout), the team cards, the weather and a twist (an injury, a sin-binned player). The situation is redrawn until the win model gives you an uphill but real chance, between 12% and 42%.

- The dice are your own: the situation is fixed, the rolls aren't (seeding them as well would let a day that starts with the CPU's ball be settled before you made a choice).
- Your **first attempt counts**. It goes on the day's shared leaderboard (wins first, by the winning margin, then defeats by how close they were), and its result stays on your phone with your past days. An attempt left unfinished counts as no result. Play again as often as you like as practice.
- **Share it**: save a score card (an image with the day, the situation, the final score and a square for every score after you took over) or copy a result line.

The leaderboard lives in the page's shared data on claude.ai, so it needs the page open there, signed in, and shared with your friends (as Contributors, so their results can be saved). Each player writes only their own entry; names come from claude.ai, not from the game.

## Friends league

Several phones, one league. One friend starts it: pick the league (Prem, URC, Top 14 or Super Rugby), your club, the season and match length, and the team cards, bench and weather. The league gets a six-letter code; friends join with it and pick their own clubs (two friends can't take the same one). When everyone's in, whoever started it kicks off the season, and the CPU takes every club nobody picked.

- Each round, play your match: against a CPU club on your own phone, or against a friend's club online, phone to phone (the home side's phone hosts; both phones just open the match and it kicks off).
- A round moves on when every friend's match in it is played; the hub shows whose match is still to come. The CPU's matches are played out on every phone with dice seeded by the league and the fixture, so every phone gets the same results without anyone sending them. Only the results of matches people played are stored.
- The table, play-offs, season stats and Team of the Season work as in any league. A friend who leaves mid-season hands their club to the CPU, so nobody waits for them.

Like the leaderboard, a friends league lives in the page's shared data on claude.ai: everyone opens the page there, signed in, with access to it as a Contributor.

## Weather and the bench

Two optional rules, switched on or off on the team sheet, in the competition setup and in the online lobby, like the team cards:

- **Weather** (off unless you turn it on). Each match gets its own conditions: rain about one match in three, wind about one in two. **Rain**: a wet ball, so the Forwards' and Centres' free ‹Pass› face is a knock-on (the Scrum Half's passes and every run are as normal; a card that stops a knock-on still does). **Wind**: kicks with the wind at your back go 10 m further, and 10 m shorter into it; the ends change at half time. The pitch shows the rain and an arrow for the wind, the pass odds and kick chances count them, and the commentary says when the weather made the difference.
- **The bench** (on unless you turn it off). Once a half, each side may bring on a replacement to clear an injured player's penalty. The choice comes when a player is hurt (any injured player may come off then) and at half time, when each side gets a fresh replacement and can use it on an old injury. Use it now, or keep it in case someone who carries more gets hurt; an unused replacement is gone at half time. The CPU brings the replacement on straight away for whoever carries most: in 40,000 simulated matches for each policy, keeping it for something worse never did better (49.6% to 49.9% against replacing at once), so it's a close call, and the CPU takes the safe one.

## Team cards

Five of twelve cards are dealt after the coin toss and each side picks **two**. The toss **loser** picks 1st and 4th; the toss winner, who also has the first ball, picks 2nd and 3rd; the fifth card is discarded. Each card bends one rule for the whole match: Sure Hands (the Outside Backs never lose it), Jackal (wins rucks on the video ref), Golden Boot (+20 m on place kicks, and the ball back after a penalty goal), Fortress (the other side's big moves are 2 lower) and so on, and a side's two cards work together (two kicking cards add up). The draft screen shows all five and the pick order; the scoreboard shows each side's two, and tapping them shows what they do. When a card changes a roll, a short scene says so.

**Rarities.** Every card is dealt at a rarity of its own: **Common** (55% of cards), **Rare** (28%), **Epic** (13%) or **Mythic** (4%). With five dealt, about one deal in five has a Mythic, and it can be the first card on offer: no catch-up, what you see is what you get. The rarer the card, the stronger it is: each rarity keeps everything below it and adds more. Sure Hands, for example: Common, the Outside Backs never lose it; Rare, their Hit Ups are +1 too; Epic, +2 and Into the Corner 10; Mythic, the Fly Half never finds touch either. Cards show their rarity with a coloured gem and frame (grey, blue, purple, and a shining orange-gold for Mythic), and the rules sheet shows all twelve at each rarity.

**Balance.** Every card at every rarity was measured over 4,000 simulated matches in each format against a side with no card. In a race to 30, Common cards lift the win rate by 7.8 to 12.5 points, Rare 12.0 to 16.5, Epic 15.7 to 19.7 and Mythic 21.3 to 27.9: every card is stronger at each rarity, and any card two rarities up beats any card below. On the clock cards count for less, since there are fewer scores to swing (Common 7.3 on average, Rare 9.3, Epic 12.1, Mythic 18.1), and the cards that work through set pieces and penalties count least of all. The win meter and the CPU's draft use the value for the format being played. With the full draft the toss loser, who picks first, won 51.6% in both formats against the toss winner's second and third picks and first ball. Cards can be turned off on the team sheet. [RULES.md](RULES.md) lists every card's exact effect at every rarity.

## Players, stats and your record

- **Named players.** Every side has five named players (edit them, or shuffle new ones, on the team sheet). Commentary uses their names: "TRY! Webb (Outside Back) dots it down!"
- **Run metres.** Every carry makes ground, read from the dice: tackled, a carrier makes twice the run's intensity less the tackle total (never below 0), so a Hit Up 5 stopped by a 5 makes 5 m and one stopped by a 9 makes 1 m; a Big Hit or a ruck contest stops it dead. A line break makes 10 m and 5 m more for each point the tackle fell short, and a try adds 10 m for the run-in. The pitch's gain line moves with the metres made. A standard match comes to about 150 m a side, 7 m a carry.
- **Player of the Match** after every match, with a spotlight scene, and a table of every player's tries, metres, carries, line breaks, tackles, missed tackles, turnovers, kicking points and errors. The team report adds metres per carry and tackle success.
- **Win meter.** A live bar under the scoreboard shows each side's chance of winning, from a model of how possessions end (it agrees with simulated matches to within a few points), with each side's team card counted as an edge worth its measured lift. The full-time report charts it through the match as a momentum graph, with every score marked.
- **Career.** Your record against each CPU level and online, streaks, points, tries, biggest win, trophies, and the team cards you've won with, each at the rarest you've had.
- **36 achievements**, from Hat-trick, Hundred Metres and Comeback to Ice Cold (win it with a kick), From Halfway (a 50 m drop goal), Giant Killer (beat the Expert), Mythic (win with a Mythic card), Champions of Europe, The Double (your league and the Champions Cup in one season), Dynasty (three league titles with one club), Late Show (win a daily challenge), Bragging Rights (win a friends league) and Invincibles (win a tournament unbeaten). Each one gets a scene when you unlock it.

## Presentation

- **3D dice** that tumble onto the result, solid cubes with a shadow on the table, with the face that matters highlighted. Rolls that decide a try, kick, card or ruck spin longer, to a heartbeat.
- **The dice guide.** Before every roll, the row under the dice shows the six faces of the die about to be rolled and what each would do, from your side's point of view: green good, red bad, grey neither, with a line-break chance on every run, the kick chance on every range, and so on. Each face is played through the rules engine, so injuries, the sin bin, the team cards, the weather and the clock all count. Tap a face for a sentence on it; when the dice land, the face they show lights up. A tackle shows the total needed and the chance of stopping the run.
- **Badges.** A die's top-right corner shows what's changing its numbers: an injury (a red cross and −1 or −2), the sin bin (a yellow card, −1 on each tackle die), a red card (−2), advantage (+1), the wind (±10 m) or a team card (+10 m and up). The same badge shows on the roll button and on a receiver's chip when you pass.
- **Auto-roll.** Rolls your dice for you, a beat apart so you can follow, and stops at every decision. Turn it on and off with the Auto button; it's on to start an online match.
- **Cinematic moments**: kick-off and second-half title cards, the Mythic card reveal, the team-card face-off, line breaks in slow motion, tries, video-ref reviews, big hits, injuries, cards, turnovers, penalties, quick taps, half time, full time, Player of the Match, achievements and the trophy lift. Every kick at goal plays on a kick cam with a distance gauge. Tap to skip any scene; set them to Full, Short or Off. They switch off when your phone asks for reduced motion.
- **Live pitch** with a camera that follows the runner, and a stadium scoreboard whose digits flip.
- **Clear decisions.** Each pass, drop-goal and penalty choice shows its odds (break chance, error chance, kick chance, with team cards included) and a tip.
- Commentary, a full match log, half-time and full-time reports.
- Quick, Standard and Marathon: 60, 100 or 160 rolls on the clock, or first to 20, 30 or 50.
- Saves after every roll, including tournaments and online matches.
- Sound, vibration, fast dice, keyboard controls (Space to roll, 1–4 to choose, Esc for the menu), and works offline once loaded.

## Online play

Online matches run through the page's live room on claude.ai, so both players open the game there:

- Both players must be **signed in** and have **access to the page**. The owner shares it from the **Share** menu. Someone who opens a public link can't join a match.
- The host picks the match type and length, team cards, the bench and the weather. The guest calls the toss.
- **Auto-roll** is on to start, so the dice keep moving. Each **decision has 10 seconds** (a clock runs on both phones): run out of time in play and the other side gets a penalty. The toss and the card picks are made for you instead (the best card on the table), and so is a roll left that long with auto-roll off.
- If both choose the same kit colour, the guest changes strip.
- Closing the page doesn't end the match: **Resume** rejoins the room and carries on from the last move.

How it works: each phone keeps the whole match and shares only its own moves, each one an action plus the random numbers its dice used. The other phone replays every move through the same rules engine, so a move that breaks the rules, comes out of turn or has the wrong dice is refused. The phones also compare a fingerprint of the match after every move, to catch any disagreement. Nothing is stored on a server: moves travel in each player's room presence, which the room re-sends to anyone who reconnects. Outside claude.ai the Online button explains this instead.

## The CPU, honestly

Expert plays every option forward to the end of the possession many times over, on the same dice for each option, and asks the win model which leaves it best placed. It rarely makes a poor call, but in a dice game the dice decide most matches. Measured with team cards on: Pro beat Rookie in 54.9% of 4,000 matches (±1.5%), Expert beat Rookie in 56.6% of 800 (±3.5%), and Expert beat Pro in 50.3% of 1,600 (±2.5%). So the levels are honest but close: good decisions are worth a few points in a hundred, not a walkover. The styles change how the CPU plays more than how often it wins.

Since a score now hands the other side the ball, the Pro (and the Tip badge) goes for goal only at a 2-in-3 chance or better, where it used to kick at evens: over 4,000 matches in each format, that won 52.8% (on the clock) and 54.4% (first to 20) against the old habit. Boot it still kicks at every chance; Run it taps unless it's a sitter. On the clock, near the end the CPU takes three points only if they draw or win the match, and in sudden death any score will do.

## What the simulations changed

The rules were tuned by playing thousands of matches CPU against CPU (the scripts are in `scripts/`):

- **The restart.** The printed rules give the ball to the side that scored. Over 2,000 matches that snowballs: the median winning margin in a race to 30 was 15 points, and 73% of matches were won by 10 or more. Giving the restart to the side that conceded made the median margin 11 (7 on the clock), made lead changes 40% to 65% more common, and turned "kick or tap" into a real choice.
- **The clock lengths** (above), and **bonus points** set for the margins matches actually produce: on the clock, 21% to 44% of wins are by 10 or more and 30% to 44% of defeats by 5 or fewer.
- **Kicking at goal** (above), and **the draft order**: picks 1 and 4 against 2 and 3 plus the first ball came out even.
- **The win model** was re-measured for the new restart and extended to the clock: from 4,000 matches, a possession ends with no points and the ball lost 49.4% of the time, no points and the ball kept 14.7%, three points 14.9%, a try 6.6% and a converted try 14.3%. Its predictions agree with simulated matches to within a few points in every tenth of the range.
- **The kicking cards.** Once a score handed over the ball, Golden Boot and Drop-Goal Artist were the weakest cards at every rarity (a Mythic Drop-Goal Artist was worth less than an Epic of anything else). Now they win the restart back after their own goals, once a match at Common and once a half above, which puts them mid-pack. Letting them keep it every time was far too strong: their Mythics won 89%. Midfield Maestro lost its drop-goal parts for bigger runs, and Squeaky Clean, Set-Piece Kings, Sure Hands and Up and Under were lifted where a rarity barely beat the one below.
- **The win meter** counts what the model can't see: the cards (at their value for the format), sin bins (a half of the other side in the bin was worth 8.5 points on the clock and 12.1 in a race), red cards (19.0 and 26.1) and injuries. The daily challenge also plays each day out, 40 seeded matches, so a day the model misjudges isn't picked.
- **Decisions** come about every 5 rolls (one in ten waits 19 or more), which is why the dice guide explains every roll in between and auto-roll can play them for you.

```sh
node scripts/eval-ai.mjs 400 expert:balanced pro:balanced   # head-to-head CPU strength
node scripts/balance-cards.mjs                                # every card against no card
```

## How a turn works

1. The **Scrum Half** rolls first after every kick-off, tackle or turnover. Most faces pass to a named player, who rolls next.
2. A run has an **intensity**. The defence rolls the brown die twice. If the total reaches the intensity, the tackle is made. If it falls short, it's a line break and the attacker rolls the black die for the try.
3. **Errors** (knock on, into touch) give the other side a scrum or lineout on the white referee die. **Intercepted** turns the ball over straight away.
4. **Penalties** check for a yellow card (sin bin: −1 on each tackle die for the half) and roll the range. Then you choose a kick at goal or a quick tap (+1 intensity for 3 runs).
5. **Kicks**: the brown die sets the range and the black die's number is the kick distance. Each point is 10 metres.

The in-game **How to play** sheet has the full rules, every face of every die and every team card.

## About the dice

The printed rules don't list every face, so the faces here are reconstructed from the rules sheet and photos of the physical set. Each player keeps the big move named in the rules (Line Break 6, Thru the Gap 7, Into the Corner 8, High Ball 6). The balance was checked by simulating thousands of CPU-vs-CPU matches:

```sh
npm run simulate     # node scripts/simulate.mjs [games] [quick|standard|long]
```

A standard race to 30 averages about 5.7 tries and 4 drop-goal chances, and the side with the first ball wins about half the time (the other side has it to start the second half). The Fly Half kicks every goal: the Centres' Drop Goal face gives the chance to the Fly Half, so the dice keep their odds and the right player takes the kick. All face definitions live in `js/dice.js`.
